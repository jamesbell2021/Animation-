Kirkhaven, October 1987. To save three keepers' wages, the Lighthouse Board puts the harbour light on a clock. The first night nobody tends it, the clock sticks, the light never lights, and a bank of fog comes in against the wind.

There is something in it.

Sea They Are Angry is a fog-horror survival game for one to three keepers, on the coast of Scotland. By day you gather, mend and prepare. At dusk the foghorn sounds, the fog rolls in glowing off the sea, and things that should have stayed under the water walk up the beaches toward the light. Keep the light burning.

The story

Kirkhaven

Three nights in a fishing town whose lights have gone out. Feed the harbour's generator, switch the street lamps back in one by one (the fog cannot lie in a burning lamp's light), work the harbour light and keep the kirk doors, where the seven of Kirkhaven shelter: the minister, the chandler, the engineer, the publican, the nurse, the harbour master and the net-mender. Each helps while they live. You cannot hold the town. On the third night you bring whoever is left down through the dark streets to the boat.

The Escape

The Mary Ann runs up the coast through the fog with Kirkhaven's people aboard, and they come up out of the water after her. Someone takes the wheel and steers her through the rocks and away from the great arms rearing out of the sea; everyone else knocks the drowned off her sides with the boathook, the bow and the shotgun.

The rock

One light on the coast is still kept by hand. On the rock, the survivors shelter in an old wartime internment camp across a swaying rope bridge, and nobody is coming to take you off. Every day brings something (a wreck on a beach, a page washed up, a low tide that opens a cave) and some nights are worse than others: storms, squid, smotherers for the lamp. The camp's diary says what came in 1944, and when it will come again. You arm.

The last night

The kraken. It takes the camp first, then comes round for the lighthouse. It cannot abide the light in its eyes.

Features

  • A whole story, about an hour long: the fall of Kirkhaven, the escape by boat, the rock, and a three-stage fight with the kraken, told between the acts in a keeper's log drawn in pencil.
  • A fog that hunts the dark. It glows, comes ashore ahead of each landing, and is held off by light. The lamp's wide beam slows them; the focused beam burns them, at the price of oil.
  • Six kinds of creature, each with its own way in: the Bloated Dead, the wall-climbing Squid, the armoured Crab, the giant Eel, the Smotherer that climbs the tower to put the light out, and the drowned that board the boat.
  • Weapons from the shed's workbench: the boathook, a driftwood longbow, the old keeper's double-barrelled shotgun, a weed burner rigged as a flamer, petrol bombs.
  • Three keepers, each with a knack: Callum hits hardest with the hook, Hamish is the shot, Mairi mends and refuels fastest.
  • Seven people to keep alive, who help while they live, ask you for things, and grieve for the ones you lose.
  • A camp to run: a diesel generator and searchlights, a wind turbine, crops under glass, a research desk, hunger and cold, a rowing boat out to Gull Rock.
  • One to three players on one screen, splitting when you go apart, with computer-played keepers filling the empty places. Or play the rock over a LAN.
  • Three seas, calm, angry and raging. Each dawn rates the night in stars; finish on the harder seas to unlock extras.
  • A cold synthesiser score in the spirit of John Carpenter's The Fog: a voice on the wireless, a knock at the door, a foghorn that isn't yours.

Controls

W A S D walk · Mouse aim · Left mouse swing or fire · F use, mend, take the wheel · R work the lamp · B wide or focused beam · Right mouse flare · Tab or 1–5 weapons · Q E turn the camera · H show more or less · Esc pause. A gamepad works too, and every key can be changed.

Co-op

Up to three people on one machine: one on keyboard and mouse, others on gamepads or the arrow-key side of the keyboard. Join a game already under way by day. The rock can also be played over a LAN, one keeper to a machine.

Accessibility

Every key can be changed. There are colour-blind friendly keeper colours, a steadier-lights option, adjustable screen shake and hit pause, a larger interface, captions for the night's sounds, and three difficulties.

Requirements

Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), or 64-bit Linux. A graphics card that supports Vulkan (most from 2016 on). About 150 MB of disk.

Feedback

Press F12 at any moment to save a snapshot of where you are (it goes in feedback.txt next to your saves), and tell us in the comments what you were doing. It helps more than anything.
