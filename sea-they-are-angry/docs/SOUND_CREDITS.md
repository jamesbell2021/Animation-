# Sound credits

Most of the game's sounds are still made in code (`autoload/audio.gd`). The ones
listed here are recordings: `audio/<key>.ogg` is played in place of the made sound
with the same key (see `_build_all()`).

Every recording below is **CC0 or public domain**. Nothing here needs attribution,
but the people who made the recordings are named anyway. The licence column is the
wording read from the source on **10 November 2026**:

- **Wikimedia Commons**: the `LicenseShortName` / `License` / `UsageTerms` fields from
  the Commons API (`action=query&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=extmetadata`), and the licence
  template on the file's own page.
- **OpenGameArt**: the "License(s)" field on the entry's page.

If a file is added or swapped, add its row here before it is committed, with the
licence read from the source page, not guessed.

## What was done to them

All of it with ffmpeg; nothing was added that is not in the table.

- Each file is a short cut from the recording, mixed to one channel at 44.1 kHz,
  with rumble below 30 Hz removed, short fades at each end of the one-shots, its
  level set against the made sound it replaces (peak no higher than -1 dBFS), and
  encoded as Ogg Vorbis (quality 5).
- The loops (`waves`, `wind`, `rain`, `generator`) are 14 to 22 s long, with the end
  crossfaded into the start so that they repeat without a join.
- `waves`, `wave_crash`: wind rumble below 90 Hz removed; on `waves` the swell is
  evened out a little (gentle compression).
- `wind`: the Fisterra recording, with the howling-wind recording 9 dB under it.
- `rain`: the left channel only; thumps below 80 Hz removed.
- `gull_*`: background below 500 Hz (700 Hz for `gull_alarm`, recorded by a
  waterfall) removed.
- `foghorn`: the diaphone's tone and the first second of its closing grunt;
  `foghorn_short`: the first second of the tone.
- `boat_horn`: one and a half seconds of the long note; wind rumble below 200 Hz removed.
- `camp_bell`: the hand bell at 0.75 times speed.
- `kirk_bell`: one stroke of the small bronze bell played at 0.24 times its speed,
  which makes it a large, slow bell. `bell`: another stroke at 0.6 times speed.
- `knock`: the last knock of each of the recording's three takes, at 0.8 times
  speed, set 0.8 s apart.
- `generator`: the mower's engine at 0.55 times speed, which makes it a slower,
  heavier engine.
- `shotgun`: a little more bass (3 dB at 140 Hz).
- `glass`, `hammer`: the two files named in the table laid over each other.
- Highest peaks limited (a few dB) on `waves`, `wave_crash`, `wind`, `rain`,
  `camp_bell`, `splash`, `hammer`, `step` and `shotgun`.

The Commons recordings were fetched as Commons' own MP3 transcodes of the files
named above (`upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/...`), because the
originals were rate-limited on the day. The transcodes are the same recordings
under the same terms.

## Looked at and not used

Left out because the licence was not CC0 or public domain, or was unclear:

- "Gunshot Sounds" on OpenGameArt (opengameart.org/content/gunshot-sounds): the page
  says CC0, but the archive holds a text file that says CC BY 3.0.
- "Solo Seagull Sound Effects" on OpenGameArt: taken from somebody else's video.
- "Storm, Thunderbolts" on Commons (File:Storm_thunderbolts.ogg): marked public domain,
  but its page says it was recorded by someone other than the uploader and put up "in
  his name". It was prepared and then taken out again; the thunder is still made in
  code. If the owner is content with that provenance it can go back in.
- Anything on Commons whose API licence was CC BY or CC BY-SA (most rain, thunder,
  church bell, ship horn and engine recordings there).
