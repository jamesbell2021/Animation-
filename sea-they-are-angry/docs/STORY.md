# The story (v2: the town first, then the rock)

Decided with the owner on 2026-11-10. This replaces the order the game shipped in at
v1.0.0 (the rock, then the town). Anything written for players (log pages, reports,
warnings, the end card) must agree with this file.

## In one paragraph

October 1987. The lighthouse Board, to save three keepers' wages, puts the harbour
light at Kirkhaven on a clock. The first night nobody tends it, the clock sticks, the
light never lights, and a bank of fog comes in against the wind with something in it.
Three keepers just paid off are in the town. They know what a light is for: they try to
put Kirkhaven's back, and to keep its people alive. They cannot hold it. On the third
night the fog comes over the quay and they get whoever is left down to the *Mary Ann*
and away to the one light on the coast the Board's engineers have not reached: the
rock. There, with the survivors sheltering in the old wartime camp, they learn from the
internees' diary what came in 1944 and when it will come again, and they spend the days
gathering everything that might hurt it. On the last night it rises: the kraken. It
takes the camp first, then comes for the lighthouse. The lamp in its eyes blinds it and
holds it while everyone throws all they have at it. When it goes down the fog goes with
it, the sky clears blue, and that is the end.

## The theme

**A light kept is a promise.** The lights on that coast were never only for ships. The
Board's clock breaks the promise; the keepers keep it by hand. The last line of the
game is that sentence.

## The acts

**Act One: Kirkhaven. "The night the lights went out."** Three nights. Feed the
generator, switch the street lamps in, work the harbour light, keep the kirk doors
(the seven shelter there at night). The fog cannot lie in a burning lamp's light. The
town cannot be held: on the third night the fog comes over the quay, the skipper calls
that he cannot lie at the pier past the ebb, and the seven have to be brought from the
kirk down through the streets to the boat. Whoever reaches her is saved. Then the
keepers board and she sails.

**Between the acts: the Escape.** (Added 2026-11-11 at the owner's request.) The *Mary
Ann* runs up the coast in the fog on the third night, for the rock, whose light sweeps
ahead and comes nearer. What was in the fog comes up out of the water after her: rocks
and great arms rearing out of the sea in her way, and the drowned and the bloated
climbing her sides. Dod Wiseman keeps the wheel unless a keeper takes it; the keepers
knock them back into the sea before they get over the gunwale and take one of the seven
over the side. When the rock's light reaches her they fall back (they cannot abide it),
and she comes in under the light. Whoever was taken on the crossing is lost, as in the
town. If her hull is broken she founders, and that run is lost.

**Act Two: the rock.** The survivors live in the camp on the holm. The game as it was
(gather, mend, the lamp, the camp, the creatures), but nobody is coming to take the
keepers off. The diary's last pages say what came in November 1944, "the size of the
holm", on the ninth night, and that "it cannot abide the light in its eyes". The days
are for arming: the shotgun, the longbows, petrol bombs, the flamer, the camp's
searchlights fuelled.

**The last night: the kraken.** Vast, dark and many-armed, with two great pale eyes: an
octopus the size of the holm. It rises off the holm, tears down the wire, the huts and
the towers, then comes round for the lighthouse. Its arms can be cut back. The
focused beam in its eyes blinds it: it stops, its arms go slack, and it can be hurt.
When it dies, the fog rolls out, the storm ends, the sky clears blue.

## The keeper's log (the sketch pages)

Pencil on a ruled notebook, one drawing and one or two lines a page, in the first
person of the head keeper. Dated entries. Plain words.

**Before Act One**

1. *Kirkhaven, October 1987.* The Board has put the harbour light on a clock. Thirty
   years I have kept a lamp, and a tin box with a dial is to do it for nothing.
2. They paid the three of us off on the Monday. We took rooms over The Creel and waited
   on the Mary Ann to carry our kit out to the rock one last time.
3. Tuesday night the clock stuck. The harbour light never lit. Nobody noticed until the
   fog came in, and it came in against the wind.
4. There was something in it.
5. The street lamps went one after another, west to east. Mr Baird got who he could
   into the kirk and barred the doors.
6. We know what a light is for. Wednesday, first light. We are going to put Kirkhaven's
   back.

**Between the acts (the crossing)**

Pages 1 and 2 as the *Mary Ann* leaves the pier; then the Escape is played; pages 3 and
4 as she comes in under the rock's light.

1. We could not hold it. The third night it came over the quay and up the wynds.
2. We got (so many) of them down to the Mary Ann. (The names of the lost, if any.)
3. Dod Wiseman put her head to the north-east. There is one light on this coast the
   Board's engineers have not reached. They came up out of the water after us all the
   way up the coast. (We kept them off her. / They took (the names) over the side.)
4. The rock. The old camp on the holm will shelter them. The lamp wants oil and a hand
   to light it, and it has both.

**The diary (on the rock, when the last pages are found)**

1. *From the internees' diary, November 1944.* It came up on the ninth night, the size
   of the holm. It took the wire and the huts.
2. The lamp was all that stopped it. It cannot abide the light in its eyes.

**The last dusk**

1. Tonight. Everything we have is on the shingle or in our hands.

**After**

1. It went down at first light, and the fog went with it.
2. The sky cleared from the west. I had forgotten the colour.
3. The Board's man came out in the spring with his tin box. We showed him this log. He
   took the box back with him.
4. *A light kept is a promise.*

## The credits

Over the end card plays an arrangement of "The Skye Boat Song", a traditional air long
out of copyright: a song about carrying someone over the sea to an island. The owner
asked for "Flower of Scotland"; it is in copyright (Roy Williamson, 1967) and cannot be
shipped without a licence. If one is had, replace `audio/music_credits.ogg`.

## What is not in it

No relief boat comes to take the keepers off the rock in the story (LAN games, which
are the rock alone, keep that ending). The town is not relit on screen: the end is the
clear sky over the rock.
